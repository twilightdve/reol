Many of you may be attending a Reol live show for the first time on the Bijigaku Tour!
Here's a guide with everything you need to know before going to a Reol concert.
Of course, everyone enjoys live shows differently, so please take this article as a general reference.

## What to Bring (For Entry)

#### Essentials

- 🎫 Ticket
  - Save your phone battery since e-tickets are common
- 💰 Cash
  - For the drink ticket. Usually around 600 yen is required at entry
  - Some venues don't accept cashless payments, so check in advance
- 💧 Drinks
  - You can exchange the drink ticket you receive at entry for a drink
  - Essential since you'll get thirsty from cheering and sweating
  - If you want to be at the very front, buy a drink beforehand and exchange your ticket after the show

#### Nice to Have

- 🔒 Small bag
  - Something compact that won't get in the way
- 🎤 Towel
  - For wiping sweat. Reol's lives get as intense as a workout
- 👂 Earplugs
  - Live-specific ear protectors are recommended
  - Recommended for those concerned about noise-induced hearing damage
- 👟 Comfortable sneakers
  - Especially important for standing shows

#### Not Needed

- 🧳 Large bags
  - They won't fit in lockers and will bother people around you — leave them at your hotel
- 🔋 Portable charger
  - Fine if you're watching quietly, but too heavy for jumping around — put it in a locker

## What to Wear

For standing shows, comfortable and easy-to-move-in clothing is the way to go. Hall shows are more relaxed, but here are some tips either way.

- Avoid platform shoes or high heels
  - Out of consideration for others and for your own safety
  - Shorter attendees may opt for platforms, but be careful of injuries
- Remove large accessories or hats
  - They can block the view for people behind you
- It tends to get hot inside venues
  - Layered clothing that's easy to take off is ideal
- Store your belongings in a coin locker to stay light
  - Staff may ask you to put away large bags

## Where to Stand

For standing shows, after entering in order of your ticket number, you can choose your spot freely — where you stand can completely change your experience. Whether you squeeze into a gap near the front or hang back, choose a spot that matches your style.

Some venues may have structural elements like pillars that block visibility or affect sound, so checking the venue layout in advance is recommended.

- **Front center area**
  - Closest to the artist with an incredible sense of immersion
  - Great for seeing Reol's facial expressions and subtle performance details
  - Can get very crowded with pushing and jostling
  - Experienced concertgoers may move into any open space, so if you don't want to give up your spot, close the gap or hold your ground (just talk it out... no fighting, please)
  - Best for those with stamina
- **Middle to back area**
  - Relatively relaxed and comfortable
  - Great view of the full stage production and lighting
  - Recommended for first-time concertgoers
- **Left/Right side areas**
  - Usually less crowded, easier to get to the front even with a late entry number
  - Great for watching band members up close
  - Some angles may have limited stage visibility
  - Be aware of being right in front of speakers where the volume can be very loud
- **(If available) 2nd floor seating**
  - Wide field of view overlooking the entire stage
  - Often has better sound balance
  - Further from the artist, so facial expressions and fine details may be harder to see

## Coin Locker Information

We've compiled coin locker info on each venue's detail page. Live houses sometimes have lockers inside the venue. Hall shows may offer a cloak service. Checking coin lockers near the venue in advance is always a good idea.

## Concert Etiquette

- 📱 No photography or recording
  - Unless officially announced otherwise
- 🗣 Be considerate during MCs and quiet parts
- 🎶 In Japan, singing along is generally considered rude unless the artist encourages it
  - Some countries have a sing-along culture, so when in Rome, do as the Romans do
- 🙋 Moshing and crowd surfing are generally prohibited
- 🎒 Always put large bags in lockers
- 🤝 Work together with fellow fans to create an enjoyable atmosphere

## How to Enjoy the Show

Everyone has their own way of enjoying a live show. Just have fun at your own pace, in your own way.

Here are some ideas for reference:

- 💡 As long as you're not bothering others, enjoy the show however you like! (No need to force yourself to match everyone else)
- 🎤 Feel the unity with the artist
- 🎶 Take in the stage production, lighting, and sound
- 🕺 Get hyped with the fans around you
- 📸 (If photography is allowed) Capture the atmosphere with photos of merch and the venue

## When to Arrive

Your ticket will show two times: "Doors Open" and "Show Start." "Doors Open" is when you can enter the venue, and "Show Start" is when the performance begins.

- **Numbered entry (standing shows)**
  - Arrive near the venue about 30 minutes before doors open
- **Reserved seating**
  - Arriving at door time is fine
- **If you want to buy merchandise**
  - If there's a must-have item, check the merch sales start time for each venue in advance and line up before it begins

## After the Show

Basking in the afterglow and sharing your thoughts at a nearby restaurant is part of the fun.
Some places may be crowded with fans after the show, so making a reservation in advance is a good idea.
We've included nearby restaurant information on each venue page, so be sure to check it out.

❗️ If you experience ringing or discomfort in your ears after the show, see an ENT doctor as soon as possible. Noise-induced hearing damage requires early treatment. If you're concerned, we recommend using the live ear protectors mentioned in the "Nice to Have" section above.
(In the worst case, you could lose your hearing, so please seek medical attention as soon as possible)
